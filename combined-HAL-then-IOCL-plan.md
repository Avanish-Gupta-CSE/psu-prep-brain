### A7. What "AIR 1" honestly requires

Straight arithmetic, no cheerleading.

**HAL.** Final merit = `0.85 × CBT% + 0.15 × Interview%`.
Your 73.5% CIL baseline maps to roughly **118/160** on a cold transfer. Target is **128/160 (80%)** — a 10-mark lift, which is exactly what the weak-area list in A4 is worth if repaired. At CBT 80% and an interview at your demonstrated 80%, composite = `68.0 + 12.0 = 80.0`. With 3 seats and a 1:5 shortlist (~15 called), the realistic honest framing is: **top-15 shortlist is highly probable; the single OBC-NCL seat is a coin-flip decided by the interview, where you are strong.** The 15% interview weight is your structural advantage — do not treat interview prep as optional.

**IOCL.** No interview weightage published for the CBT stage; merit order off 100 marks.
Target **80/100** = 60/75 Section A + 20/25 Section B. Gates (30/75, 10/25, 45/100) are not in question. With 22 posts and 4 OBC-NCL seats, this is the **wider funnel and the better expected value**. It also has 18 days of preparation behind it instead of 4.5.

**The honest sequencing call:** HAL is a 4.5-day tactical strike from a 73.5% base. IOCL is where AIR-1-grade preparation is actually possible, because you get the full month. Run HAL hard, take the shortlist, then convert the entire remaining month into IOCL — which is precisely what the stacking design below does.

**Before Q1:** read the on-screen instructions and settle **R3** — is there negative marking? Set your mode:

- **Zero negative** → attempt all 160. Never leave a blank.
- **Negative marking** → attempt only above roughly 50% confidence; leave pure blind guesses.

**Pass structure (150 min for 160 Q):**

| Pass | Window | Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | 0–15 min | **Part I GA (20 Q)** — pure recall. Under 30 s each. Do not linger. |
| 2 | 15–50 min | **Part II English + Reasoning (40 Q)** — bank the easy accuracy. Flag any puzzle over 90 s and move. |
| 3 | 50–125 min | **Part III CS (100 Q)** — first sweep theory and one-liners, flag every multi-step numerical. Then return for the flagged numericals. |
| 4 | 125–145 min | Flagged questions, educated elimination |
| 5 | 145–150 min | Confirm zero unattempted (if no negative marking); confirm nothing is accidentally left marked-for-review-only |

**Hard rule:** no question gets more than 2 minutes on the first sweep. At 56 s average, one 5-minute question costs you four easy marks elsewhere.

### D5. Error-log protocol

Every wrong answer is classified. Errors have different cures and mixing them wastes time.

| Class | Meaning | Cure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `concept` | Did not know it | Re-study the sub-topic, then 10 fresh questions |
| `computation` | Knew it, arithmetic slipped | Slow down 10%; write intermediate steps |
| `trap` | Fell for a distractor | Collect the trap pattern; drill "which is NOT true" questions |
| `time` | Knew it, ran out of clock | Pacing problem, not knowledge — practise the pass structure |
| `misread` | Misread the question | Force a re-read of the stem before answering |

If `time` and `misread` dominate, **stop studying content and fix pacing.** That is worth more marks than any new topic in the final week.
